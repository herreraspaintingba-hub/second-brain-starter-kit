Welcome

A brain outside your head

What this kit is, in one page.

Every day you learn things: a client's habit, a price that worked, a decision and why you made it, a lesson that cost you money. Most of it is gone in a month. A second brain is a place where that knowledge lands, organized, connected, and easy to find again.

This kit turns the AI assistant you already use into the librarian of that place. You talk to it the way you always do. At the end, you say one sentence, and it files what was worth keeping. Next week, when you ask a question, it looks in your notes before asking you.

The nine skills

SkillIts one job
second-brain-initBuilds your brain the first time (or upgrades a v1 brain)
save-to-obsidianSaves what you learned, with clean links and a session log
brain-searchSearches your notes before answering or asking you
project-doorKeeps one page per project with where it stands today
session-checkpointA save point during long sessions
close-sessionCloses a working session so nothing is lost
brain-healthA monthly checkup with a traffic light and a history
project-advisorScores a new idea before you spend time on it
obsidian-power-userWorks behind the others so every note is clean and linked

The promise

  1. A brain that is set up: a vault on your computer, organized into clear sections.
  2. A capture habit: end any conversation with "save to my brain" and trust the good parts are kept.
  3. Recall without asking: the AI finds what you already decided instead of asking you again.
  4. Projects that pick up where they left off, even after weeks away.
  5. A brain that stays healthy, measured the same way every month.
How long will this take?Setup is about 10 minutes. Reading this manual, about an hour. Building the habit, a week. After that, it just works.

Step 1. Install with one command

Open the Terminal (Mac: press Cmd + Space, type "Terminal") and paste:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/herreraspaintingba-hub/second-brain-starter-kit/main/install.sh | bash

It downloads the kit, finds your assistant (Claude, Codex or Gemini) and copies the nine skills into its skills folder. If a skill was already there, the old copy is moved aside, never deleted. At the end it tells you whether Python was found.

Your assistantWhere the skills landTo choose it yourself
Claude Code, Cowork~/.claude/skills/HOST=claude
Codex~/.codex/skills/HOST=codex
Gemini CLI~/.gemini/skills/HOST=gemini
OtherCopy the nine folders from skills/ to wherever your assistant loads skills from
The ~ symbolIt is shorthand for your home folder (for example /Users/yourname on a Mac). Your file manager opens there by default.

Step 2. Say the sentence

Open a new conversation with your assistant and type:

You say"Set up my second brain."

That wakes up second-brain-init, which runs the whole setup. It does not have to be exact: "I just installed the starter kit" works too, and so does "Configura mi segundo cerebro".

What happens behind the scenesThe assistant sees there is no brain yet (no CLAUDE.md settings file), so it runs setup instead of guessing. This happens once. Saying the sentence again later only confirms you are set up.

What setup created for you

CLAUDE.md

Your brain's settings: your name, where the vault lives, your language, the rules, and the phrases. The assistant reads it at the start of every conversation.

CURRENT-CONTEXT.md

A short, living summary of what you are working on right now. Update it about once a week. It is the first note the brain reads.

Home and Start Here

Home.md and 09 MOCs/MOC, Start Here give the graph a center, so the first notes you save are never alone.

.brain/

The toolbox: the search index, the note checker, the Door reader, the health check, and vocabulary.json, the list of note types.

Your setup got saved tooThe first thing your brain does after setup is write a session log of the setup itself. Its birth certificate is already in 06 Session Logs/.

Upgrading from version 1

If you set up your brain with v1 of this kit, you keep everything. Run the install command again (it moves your old skill copies aside), then say:

You say"Upgrade my brain to v2."
  1. It adds the new folders: References, 07 Assets, 08 Projects, 09 MOCs, Archives and .brain.
  2. It puts the tools in .brain/ and builds the first search index.
  3. It rewrites CLAUDE.md with the v2 template, keeping every line you added.
  4. It runs the first health check and shows you the light.
Do not panic at the first lightAn older vault usually starts yellow or red: broken links and notes without the new properties pile up over months. That first number is your baseline, not a grade. The brain compares every month with the one before, and old notes get fixed as you touch them.

A Map of Content: a note that lists and links everything about one theme. The table of contents of a topic.

How your AI reads the brain: three layers

An AI's memory in one conversation is limited. Loading your whole vault at the start would fill it with history and leave less room for the work. So the brain reads in layers, set up in your CLAUDE.md:

LAYER 1 · ALWAYS

Who and what now

CLAUDE.md and CURRENT-CONTEXT.md. Short on purpose: they are read every single time.

LAYER 2 · BY TOPIC

What this is about

A project mentioned? Its Door front page. A past fact? A search. A note to write? The formatting rules.

LAYER 3 · ON DEMAND

Only if needed

The rest of a Door, checkpoints, old session logs, MOCs. Read when the task asks for them.

Why it matters to youLong conversations stay sharp for longer, and the AI starts each session already knowing where things stand.

3Saving knowledge

The capture habit. The single most important page in this manual.

Have a normal conversation: plan a project, work through a problem, think out loud. At the end, say:

You say"Save to my brain." · "Guárdalo en mi cerebro."

The save-to-obsidian skill makes two things from the conversation:

1 · KNOWLEDGE

Knowledge nuggets

The pieces worth having in three months: a lesson, a decision and its reasons, an idea, something about a person. Each one filed in its drawer and linked to related notes.

2 · RECORD

A session log

One record of the whole session in 06 Session Logs/: what you set out to do, what got done, the decisions, the tools, and what is still open.

What happens when you say it

1

Re-reads

The whole conversation, from the top

2

Filters

"Useful in three months?" Only yes survives

3

Searches

A note on it already? Update it, no copies

4

Writes and links

Right drawer, working links, a MOC entry

5

Checks

Every note passes the property check

When the brain saves, and when it does not

✓ It saves

  • When you ask, in any words.
  • When it offers once, at the end of a session that produced something real (a decision, a lesson, a new process), and you say yes.

✕ It does not

  • In the middle of a session "just in case".
  • Every time a task finishes.
  • More than one offer per session.
Golden rule: save on purposeA brain full of everything is as useless as an empty one. Saving only what is worth it keeps search sharp and your notes worth reading.
Doing the same thing three times?The brain will offer to turn it into a skill of its own instead of saving it again. Repeated work is a sign of a missing tool.

The rules that keep every note clean

A vault does not break in one day. It rots slowly: a renamed note leaves dead links, a quick note has no description, a copy is left behind. Version 2 writes every note by these rules so that never starts.

LINKS

Every link works

  • No file names with # ^ [ ] | : (Obsidian cannot link them).
  • Before writing [[A Note]], the brain checks that note exists.
  • No "placeholder" links to notes that do not exist yet.
  • Every new note gets at least one link pointing to it, usually from a MOC.
PROPERTIES

Every note has the same header

  • A type, a date and the real time from the clock.
  • A one-line description, written for a stranger.
  • Tags in lowercase, and a status (active, done, parked...).
  • A script checks each note before it counts as saved.

This is what that header (Obsidian calls it "properties") looks like:

---
title: "Decision, Cedar raised beds"
type: decision
date: 2026-10-02
time: "14:35"
description: "We chose cedar raised beds over in-ground rows; cost and back pain."
tags: [garden, decisions]
status: active
---
Why so strict?Search ranks the title and description highest, filters use the type and the date, and the health check trusts the status. When every note has the same header, all of that just works. The list of allowed types lives in .brain/vocabulary.json; add your own whenever you need.

Never delete, never copy

  • One note per topic. New knowledge updates the existing note; no "v2" files.
  • Nothing is deleted. A note that is no longer true moves to Archives/ and is marked superseded.
  • No backup copies inside the vault. A file like note.md.bak confuses search and links; backups go to Archives/Backups/.

Knowledge moves up

A good brain promotes what repeats. A saved article you keep coming back to graduates to a real note. When five notes keep circling the same principle, the brain writes that principle once, as its own note, and links it from a MOC. When a theme grows past about seven notes, it proposes a MOC for it.

A real save, start to finish

Ana spent forty minutes with her AI planning a vegetable garden: bed material, the watering routine, and a mistake from last year (she planted tomatoes too early and lost them to frost). She said "save to my brain". This is what came back:

Chapter 4

4Finding things again

A brain you have to remember to look in is just a folder. This one looks for you.

Golden rule: search before askingWhen the AI needs a fact about your life or work (a decision, a price, a person, how you did something), it searches your notes first. It only asks you when the brain truly has nothing.

Ask the way you talk

You say"What did we decide about the fence?"
You say"Have we done something like this before?"
You say"¿Qué sé de la familia Ortiz?"
You say"Find that article about pricing I saved."

The brain-search skill searches, opens the top notes, and answers with what they say, naming each note so you can open it. If two notes disagree, it shows you both, newest first, instead of picking one quietly.

How it finds things

Setup built a search index of your whole vault inside .brain/. Think of it as the index at the back of a book, rebuilt in seconds whenever the brain saves or closes a session.

Titles weigh more

A word in the title, the description or the tags counts more than the same word buried in the text. That is why descriptions matter.

Accents do not matter

"decision" finds "decisión", and "cafe" finds "café". Search in English or Spanish.

All words, then any

It first looks for notes with every word. If none exist, it tries any of them and tells you so.

Recipes the AI uses for you

You askWhat it does
"What did we decide about X?"Searches decisions with X in the title, newest first
"Where did we leave project Y?"Opens the project's Door first (Chapter 5), then recent session logs
"Have we done this before?"Searches session logs and lessons for the key words
"What do I know about Z?"Searches people notes first, then everything

What a good answer looks like

You: What did we decide about the fence?

AI: Searched the brain for "fence": 3 notes, I read the first two. On September 12 you decided on a cedar fence, 6 feet, along the back line only, because the neighbor is sharing the cost ([[Decision, Back fence]]). The quote was $4,200 ([[2026-09-12 Session, Fence quotes]]). One older note from August talked about vinyl; the September decision replaced it.

  • It says where it looked, in one line.
  • It answers from the notes and names each one as a link.
  • It points out the older note that disagrees, instead of hiding it.

No Python? It still searches

Without Python, the skill falls back to a plain text search of your vault: file names first, then the description lines, then the text. Same rule, same order, just less ranking. Install Python whenever you like; the index is built automatically the next time you save.

Make your notes easy to find

  • Good titles. "Decision, Back fence" beats "Thoughts 3".
  • A real description. One line a stranger would understand. The brain writes it, and it is the line search trusts most.
  • Aliases. Other names for the same thing ("the Ortiz job", "Ortiz kitchen") go in the aliases property.
When the brain asks you anywayIt means the search came up empty. Answer, and if the fact will matter again, let it save it. Next time it will not ask.
python3 .brain/tools/brain_search.py "fence" --type decision --sort date   # what the AI runs, if you are curious

Chapter 4 recap

  • The AI searches your notes before asking you.
  • Ask in plain words, in English or Spanish.
  • Answers name the notes they come from, and show disagreements.
  • Titles and descriptions are what make notes findable.
Chapter 5

5Working on projects

How a project picks up exactly where it left off, even after weeks away.

Without help, resuming a project means digging through old conversations and guessing which one is current. Version 2 gives every project three tools: a Door to open it, checkpoints while you work, and a close when you stop.

The Door: one page per project

A Door is a note in 09 MOCs/ called Door, <Project>. It says where the project stands today, in one screen, and it is the first thing the brain reads when you start working on that project.

#SectionWhat it holds
1Current state3 to 6 lines of what is true today
2Numbers that matterThe few figures that drive decisions, with their source
3Next stepsCheckboxes, and who owns each
4How it runsHow to operate it. Read only when needed
5Where everything livesLinks to the MOC, decisions, files
6HistoryOne line per change, newest first

Sections 1 to 3 are the front page: that is all the brain reads to start. The rest waits until it is needed.

The brain opens the Door, tells you in one line where things stand and what is next, and gets to work. If the Door was last checked more than a week ago, it says so and checks the numbers before deciding anything.

Starting something new?Say "new project: kitchen remodel". The brain creates its Door, adds the project to your list, and links it from a MOC so it is never an orphan.

Checkpoints: a save point while you work

In a long conversation, the AI eventually compresses the beginning to make room, a bit like keeping only the summary of the first chapters of a book. Details blur. A checkpoint is a save point, like in a video game: every 15 to 20 exchanges, at each milestone, and before any heavy step, the brain writes where the work stands to 06 Session Logs/Active Session State, <Project>.

One file per project

Each project has its own checkpoint, overwritten each time. Two projects in one day never erase each other.

After a compression

The brain re-reads the checkpoint and tells you: "the conversation was compressed; we are at...". Then it carries on. You do nothing.

Optional: a safe commit of the vault

Golden rule: freeze, then writeAll five steps describe the same moment. The brain decides what is true now, then writes it everywhere, so the checkpoint, the Door and the log never tell three different stories.

You get a five-line summary:

Session closed:
- Checkpoint: Active Session State, Garden (updated)
- Door: Door, Garden (verified today; next step: build bed 4)

About the git stepOnly if your vault is a git repository (a technical way to keep versions). It pulls changes from your other computer only when they line up cleanly; if they do not, it stops and tells you instead of mixing them. It is the only thing that commits the vault, so turn off any plugin that commits on its own.

Chapter 6

6Deciding what to build

The idea filter. Before you build anything, run it through this.

Every "yes" to a new project is a silent "no" to something else. Ideas feel exciting in the moment, and excitement is a terrible judge of what is worth building now. The project-advisor skill is a quick, honest filter that catches every idea before it eats your week.

You say"Should I do this?" · "¿Vale la pena este proyecto?"
1

Reads your brain

2

Asks up to 7

One question at a time

3

Scores 4

Weighted, math shown

4

Verdict

Green, yellow or red, written up

The seven questions

#The questionWhat it is really asking
1In one sentence, what is this?Can you say it clearly? Fuzzy ideas are not ready
2What problem does it solve, and whose?A real problem, or just novelty?
3How is it handled today?Does it replace painful manual work, or invent new work?
4What does success look like in 90 days?If you cannot name a number, it is not ready
5What will it cost to build?Time, money, tools. A rough guess is fine
6If you build this, what does not get built?The most important one. Every yes is a no
7Which part of your life or work does it strengthen?Only asked if still unclear

The scorecard

DimensionWeight
Systemization: turns repeated work into a system?25%

7.5 to 10GREEN: build it

Two honesty rulesOverride: North Star 9 or 10 and a total of at least 5.0 bumps it to green. Veto: Timing 1 or 2 caps it at yellow, however high the rest is.

Chapter 8

8Keeping it healthy

A brain rots quietly. Measured once a month with the same rules, it is caught while it is small.

None of the problems below hurts on the day it happens: a renamed note leaves a dead link, a quick save leaves an orphan, a copy is left behind. Together, over months, they turn a brain into a junk drawer. The brain-health skill measures nine things, the same way every month, and keeps the history.

It runs by itself, once a month

If you said yes to question 7 at setup, the check runs on the 1st of every month at 07:00, rebuilds the search index, and adds one row to 05 AI System/Brain Health History. Nothing else is touched. To see it or change it:

Behind the scenes: macOS uses launchd, Linux uses cron, and on Windows the brain gives you one line to paste. On a Mac with the vault in iCloud, the first run may ask for Full Disk Access for Python (System Settings, Privacy and Security); the brain tells you if so.

Chapter 9

9Troubleshooting and FAQ

There is little to break. Here is what people occasionally hit, with the fix.

"The brain cannot find my vault."

The vault folder is not connected to your assistant in this session. In Cowork, grant access to the folder; elsewhere, open the assistant in or near the vault. The brain never writes notes somewhere random as a workaround; it stops and asks.

"It did not trigger when I said the phrase."

Say it more directly: "Save this to my brain." Still nothing? The skills may be in the wrong folder (Chapter 1, Step 1). Run the install command again and start a fresh conversation.

"Search says there is no index."

Ask: "rebuild the search index." It takes seconds. If it says Python or FTS5 is missing, install Python from python.org; until then search uses plain text.

"The property check fails on my notes."

Normal for notes written by hand or before v2. Ask "fix the properties of this note". If a type or project is new, add it to .brain/vocabulary.json (the brain can do it).

"The monthly check never ran."

Ask "is the monthly check on?" The brain shows the status and the last log lines. On a Mac with iCloud, give Python Full Disk Access. If the computer was off on the 1st, macOS runs it when it wakes.

"The close stopped at the git step."

Your vault changed on two computers and the changes do not line up. The brain stopped on purpose so nothing gets mixed up. Ask it to show you the difference and decide together.

"It answered in the wrong language."

Tell it: "answer in English" or "responde en español". For good, change the language line in CLAUDE.md.

"I ran setup twice."

No harm. Setup sees your brain exists and asks: keep it, change some answers, or start over. Choose keep.

"I do not have Obsidian."

Your brain still works: notes are plain text files. Install Obsidian (free) whenever you want the graph and clickable links.

"Do I have to use Notion?"

No. It only touches the idea filter, and only if you turned it on.

When in doubt, ask the brain"Why did my save not work?" or "Is my vault connected?" usually gets a straight answer and a fix faster than this page.

Quick FAQ

Will it work on my phone?Your notes sync if the vault is in iCloud, OneDrive or Dropbox. The skills run wherever you use your assistant.
Can I rename the folders?Yes. Keep the number in front (01, 02...) and everything keeps working.
Is my data private?Yes. Everything stays in your vault, on your computer or your cloud drive, in plain files.
Does anything run in the background?Only the monthly health check, and only if you said yes.
What if I stop using the kit?You keep every note, forever.
Can I share the kit?Yes, that is the point. It is free under the MIT license.
Chapter 10

10Growing your brain

You started with scattered knowledge. You end with a system that remembers, finds, and looks after itself.

Name your North Star

After a few weeks, write in CLAUDE.md the one outcome you are building toward. It sharpens the idea filter more than anything else.

Teach it your world

Add the people who matter, your vocabulary and nicknames to CLAUDE.md. Keep it short; move detail into notes and link them.

Add your own types

Track recipes, clients, properties? Add a type to .brain/vocabulary.json and every tool follows.

Build a pack for your trade

The same base can learn a trade: painters, real estate, consulting, restaurants. Use it a few weeks first; you will see what your trade needs.

The best way to learn it is to teach itWalk a friend through setup. It takes ten minutes, and now there are two of you with a second brain.

A final word

You do not have to be technical to think clearly, decide well and remember what matters. The hard part was having a place for it all. Now you do. Start small: save one conversation today, open one project through its Door, and let the brain take care of the rest.
